What is "asynchronous" and why does it matter?
Restaurant analogy: You place your food order (call fetch). The waiter takes it to the kitchen (sends request). You don't stand frozen at the counter — you go back to your table and chat with friends (JS keeps running other code). When your food is ready (server responds), the waiter brings it to you (data arrives, JS updates the page).
The key insight: JavaScript is a single-threaded language — it can only do one thing at a time. But fetch() is handled by the browser's networking engine (a different thread). So while the network request is in flight, your JS code is free to do other work. await simply says "when this network thing is done, come back and continue from here."
If network requests were synchronous (blocking), your entire webpage would freeze for every 100ms network delay. Every button, animation, and scroll would stop. This is why async exists.
